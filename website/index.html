The problem

Strong institutions, fragile records.

Savings groups (VICOBA, chamas, SACCO-style circles) are small financial institutions. Members contribute, borrow, vote and share profits. Yet their records are spread across notebooks, spreadsheets, mobile-money messages, chat groups and the memory of a few leaders.

That leads to disputed balances, slow reporting, weak audit trails and loan decisions that are hard to explain. Leaders spend more time keeping books than serving their group.

The gap is not willingness to save or cooperate. It is trustworthy infrastructure built around how community finance actually works.

The platform

Everything a savings group runs on, in one system.

AtLast works as the group's ledger, passbook, loan desk, voting room, reporting centre and reconciliation tool. Members use it on their phones; leaders get the controls they need.

Membership

New members apply and are admitted only by a secret-ballot vote of the group, never by one leader alone.

Contributions & shares

Members record each payment with its M-Pesa, Airtel Money, Tigo Pesa or cash reference. Shares are earned automatically from contributions.

Loans

Eligibility is computed by the system. Each request is reviewed by three randomly chosen members with a clean repayment record. Interest and late penalties follow rates the group has set and locked.

Jamii welfare fund

A separate pot for members who fall ill, kept apart from savings. Payouts need approval from three members in good standing.

Governance

Motions, secret-ballot votes and a permanent record of past decisions, so the group's memory doesn't depend on any one person.

Reports & statements

Group health score, trends, financial dashboard, and PDF statements for every member and for the group.

Reconciliation

Leaders compare the money actually held against what the ledger says it should be, and investigate any difference on the record.

Year-end share-out

Profit from interest and penalties, less group expenses, is split by shares. The Treasurer prepares it; the Group Admin approves it.

Notices & complaints

Announcements, in-app notifications, and a private channel for members to raise complaints with their leaders.

Trust architecture

Trust is not a feature added later. It is the foundation.

01

An append-only ledger

Balances are never stored as a number someone can change. They are always calculated from the full history of transactions. Mistakes are corrected by a visible reversal, never by editing or deleting.

02

A number for every transaction

Every entry receives a permanent transaction number (for example ATL-2611-7K3M9Q), shown on receipts, statements and the ledger, kept alongside the mobile-money provider's own reference.

03

Two people on the money

The Group Admin and the Treasurer must be different people. The Treasurer pays out and prepares; the Admin approves. Neither can complete a sensitive action alone.

04

The Three-Eye Panel

Loans are approved by three randomly selected members with a proven repayment record, excluding anyone with a conflict of interest. Reviewers stay anonymous to the borrower.

05

Locked group rules

Contribution amounts, share value, interest, penalties and the Jamii amount are locked. Changing them needs a member vote and independent approval. A database guard blocks every other path.

06

Secret from people, accountable to the system

Votes are confidential, so even leaders see only totals, yet each vote is recorded once per member for audit. Discretionary and corrective actions are written to an audit log with who, when and why.

Security underneath: phone-number sign-in with one-time codes, a personal PIN on each device, role-based access enforced in the database itself, and every money-moving action handled on the server, never trusted to the phone.

For regulators

A clear line between recording money and holding it.

Today AtLast is record-keeping and governance software for savings groups. AtLast does not hold members' funds, does not lend its own money and does not move money. Groups keep their funds as they do now, in mobile money, a bank account or cash, and use AtLast to record each payment and decision transparently.

  • We keep a strict distinction between recording a transaction, initiating a payment, holding funds, lending and providing regulated financial services.
  • Any future payment or financial-service features will be offered through licensed partners, or under licences we obtain, within the laws of each country we serve.
  • We will seek jurisdiction-specific legal and regulatory review, including for data protection, before any such features launch.
  • Members within a group can see every transaction and loan of that group. Transparency is a product principle, not an exception.

We welcome early dialogue with regulators, cooperative authorities and data-protection bodies, and will gladly demonstrate the audit trail in detail.

Responsible AI

Intelligence that adds to human capability, never replaces human responsibility.

In future, AI may help explain reports, summarise meetings, translate complex information into plain language and assist reconciliation. It will not silently make financial or governance decisions. Consequential actions will always require human authority, clear controls and a traceable record.

Why AtLast

AtLast began with a simple observation: people do not need to be taught to grow together. Across Tanzania, communities already save, lend, govern and support one another through their savings groups.

What they often lack is infrastructure that reflects the way they actually live and organise. Deniss founded AtLast to provide it, not to replace community institutions, but to strengthen them with visibility, accountability and simplicity.
